# Natural Conversation Rules

## Goal

Make AI characters feel like they are genuinely participating in the same conversation rather than taking turns playing isolated voice clips.

## The listener must stay alive

The non-speaking character should not freeze completely. Use subtle listening behavior such as:

- blinking
- small nods
- tiny head tilts
- eye-line changes
- breathing-like body motion
- slight posture shifts
- restrained facial reactions

Avoid constant large movement. Listening motion should usually be smaller than speaking motion.

## Pauses and timing

Perfectly back-to-back lines often feel synthetic. Add natural timing variation.

Useful starting points:

- short response gap: about 0.2–0.6 seconds
- thoughtful response: longer than a normal response gap
- surprise reaction: sometimes almost immediate
- interruption: allow slight overlap when appropriate

Do not apply the same gap to every line.

## Speech rhythm

Avoid making every line equally clean and complete. Natural dialogue can include:

- short acknowledgements
- hesitation
- sentence fragments
- quick corrections
- emotional exclamations
- brief interjections

Examples of useful Japanese conversational fillers include 「えっ」「うん」「いや」「たしかに」「でも」「ちょっと待って」. Use them only when they fit the character.

## Blinking

Blinking should feel irregular. Avoid a visible fixed interval.

- do not blink continuously at the same cadence
- avoid blinking exactly at every sentence boundary
- occasional longer gaps feel more natural

## Head and body movement

Movement should support the line, not compete with it.

Speaking character:
- slightly larger head and upper-body motion
- emphasis on key words
- occasional posture change

Listening character:
- smaller motion
- nods at agreement points
- brief reaction after surprising statements

## Eye line

If two characters are meant to be in the same scene, their gaze direction should imply they are looking toward each other whenever possible.

If direct gaze control is unavailable, use framing and shot selection to hide mismatches.

## Facial expression

Do not keep the same expression across an entire conversation. Define a small usable expression set such as:

- neutral
- smile
- surprise
- concern
- confusion
- strong reaction

Expression changes should follow meaning, not happen randomly.

## Camera behavior

A conversation feels more natural when the edit reacts to the dialogue.

Useful shots:

- two-shot for setup or shared reaction
- speaker close-up for important lines
- listener reaction shot after a punchline or reveal
- tighter crop for emphasis

Avoid cutting on every single sentence. Cut when the information, emotion or rhythm changes.

## Reaction shots

Reaction shots are especially valuable when the animation itself is limited.

After a joke, reveal, insult, surprise or awkward statement, briefly show the listener before continuing. This gives the viewer time to process the moment and makes the interaction feel shared.

## Common causes of unnatural footage

- only the mouth moves
- listener is completely static
- both characters move at the same intensity
- every line has the same pause length
- no overlap or interjections ever occur
- identical facial expression throughout
- camera never changes
- character gaze directions do not make spatial sense
- animation loops are too obvious

## Review checklist

Before export, watch once with the sound on and once with the sound off.

With sound on, ask:
- Does the dialogue rhythm feel human?
- Are there enough reactions?
- Are pauses too uniform?

With sound off, ask:
- Can I still tell who is speaking and who is listening?
- Does anyone look frozen?
- Are repeated loops obvious?

The target is not perfect realism. The target is a believable, watchable interaction.
